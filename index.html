Finding a bearing from a vector

The three-step method

Step 1 — Sketch: Draw the vector on a compass diagram. Identify which quadrant it lies in.

Step 2 — Reference angle: Use tan to find the angle α between the vector and the nearest N–S line.

Step 3 — Bearing: Bearings are measured clockwise from North (000°). Add or adjust based on the quadrant.

Worked example from your notes: v = −i − j

Step 1 — Sketch

v = −i − j goes 1 unit West and 1 unit South → bottom-left (SW) quadrant.

Step 2 — Reference angle

tan(α) = opposite/adjacent = 1/1 = 1

α = arctan(1) = 45°

Step 3 — Bearing

South = 180°. Add our 45° past South:

Bearing = 180° + 45° = 225°

Quadrant bearing rules

NE quadrant (+i, +j): bearing = α

SE quadrant (+i, −j): bearing = 180° − α

SW quadrant (−i, −j): bearing = 180° + α

NW quadrant (−i, +j): bearing = 360° − α

Finding α — always positive

Always use the magnitude of the components:

α = arctan(|x-comp| / |y-comp|)

This gives the angle from the N–S axis (not E–W).

Watch out: tan uses |horizontal| / |vertical| when measuring from North, which is opposite to standard trig convention. Always sketch first.

Finding a magnitude (Pythagoras in 2D)

The formula

|v| = √(a² + b²) where v = ai + bj

This is just Pythagoras on the right-angled triangle formed by the components.

Worked example from your notes: v = 5i − 12j

|v| = √(5² + (−12)²)

= √(25 + 144)

= √169

= 13

Common error: (−12)² = 144, not −144. Always square the negative sign away.

Speed from a displacement vector

When i and j represent East and North (in km or m), speed = distance ÷ time.

distance = |displacement vector|

Convert time to a single unit first (e.g. 2h 45min = 2.75 hours).

The triangle inequality: |a + b| = |a| + |b|

What does it mean geometrically?

When you add two vectors tip-to-tail, you normally get a triangle — and the resultant is shorter than the two sides added together.

But if |a + b| = |a| + |b|, there is no triangle. The path is a straight line — you never turned. This means a and b are parallel and in the same direction.

Exam answer: "a and b are parallel and in the same direction" (or equivalently "a and b are collinear and point the same way").

The displacement rule: finding |m − n|

The key geometric insight

To find m − n, rewrite it as m + (−n). Then place the vectors tip-to-tail to form a triangle.

The angle inside the triangle (between m and −n) is 180° − θ, where θ is the original angle between m and n.

Which rule to use?

Is the triangle right-angled? → Pythagoras or trig ratios

Non-right: do you know a side and its opposite angle? → Sine Rule

Non-right: no side-angle pair? → Cosine Rule

The sine rule

sin A / a = sin B / b = sin C / c

Use when you know one side-angle pair and want another angle.

Worked example: |m| = 3, |m−n| = 6, angle between m and n = 30°

This is the exact Question 2(ii) from your past paper.

Step 1 — internal angle = 180° − 30° = 150°
Step 2 — sine rule to find small angle θ

sinθ / |m| = sin(150°) / |m−n|

sinθ / 3 = sin30° / 6 = 0.5 / 6

sinθ = 3 × 0.5 / 6 = 0.25 → θ = arcsin(0.25) = 14.5°

Step 3 — find the required angle (angles sum to 180°)

α = 180° − 30° − 14.5° = 135.5°

Vector geometry: position vectors & proofs

Key formulas

Vector from A to B: AB⃗ = b − a (position vector of B minus position vector of A)

Midpoint M of AB: m = ½(a + b)

Collinearity: show AB⃗ = λ AC⃗ — they share point A and are parallel, so all three points lie on one line.

Proving a point lies on a line — the parametric method

Write ON⃗ two ways: via C and via O, with a scalar parameter λ. Equate coefficients of a and b to find λ, then confirm both equations are satisfied.

This is the method used in Questions 6 and 7 of your past paper.

Proving a path passes through a point

Show that OA⃗ = k · OB⃗ for some scalar k. If they share the origin O and are parallel, the path from A through O to B is a straight line — so O is on the path.

Example: OA⃗ = −24i − 10j = 2(−12i − 5j) and OB⃗ = 12i + 5j, so AO⃗ = 2OB⃗. Parallel and sharing O → stone passes through O. ✓

Q3 — triangle inequality & sine rule

|m| = 3, |m−n| = 6, angle between m and n = 30°

5 marks

(i) Two non-zero vectors a and b are such that |a + b| = |a| + |b|. Explain, geometrically, the significance of this statement. (1 mark)

(ii) Find the angle between vector m and vector m − n, to one decimal place. (4 marks)

Angle (m, m−n) =
Part (i)

a and b are parallel and in the same direction. The tip-to-tail path is a straight line — no turning occurs, so the resultant has length equal to the sum of the individual lengths.

Part (ii) — Step 1: internal angle

Angle between m and n is 30°. Internal angle of the displacement triangle = 180° − 30° = 150°.

Step 2 — sine rule for small angle θ at vertex where m−n meets −n

sinθ / |m| = sin(150°) / |m−n|

sinθ / 3 = sin30° / 6 → sinθ = 3 × 0.5 / 6 = 0.25

θ = arcsin(0.25) = 14.5°

Step 3 — required angle

Angles in triangle sum to 180°:

α = 180° − 30° − 14.5° = 135.5°

Q4 — bearing & speed

Coastguard boat problem

6 marks

i and j are due East and due North respectively. At 10:00 the boat is at (4i − 2j) km. At 12:45 the boat is at (−3i − 5j) km. Constant speed in a straight line.

(a) Find the bearing of travel, to 1 d.p. (3 marks)

(b) Find the speed in km h⁻¹ (3 marks)

Bearing =
Step 1 — displacement vector

d = end − start = (−3i − 5j) − (4i − 2j) = −7i − 3j

Goes 7 units West, 3 units South → SW quadrant.

Step 2 — reference angle from South

θ = arctan(7/3) = arctan(2.333) = 66.8°

Bearing = 180° + 66.8° = 246.8°

Part (b) — speed

Distance = |d| = √(7² + 3²) = √(49 + 9) = √58 km

Time = 12:45 − 10:00 = 2h 45min = 2.75 hours

Speed = √58 / 2.75 ≈ 2.77 km h⁻¹

Q5 — passes through origin

Stone on ice: A(−24i − 10j), B(12i + 5j), 4 seconds

5 marks

(a) Prove that the stone passes through O. (2 marks)

(b) Calculate the speed of the stone. (3 marks)

Part (a) — show AO⃗ and OB⃗ are parallel

OA⃗ = −24i − 10j, so AO⃗ = 24i + 10j

OB⃗ = 12i + 5j

AO⃗ = 2(12i + 5j) = 2 · OB⃗

Since AO⃗ is parallel to OB⃗ and they share point O, the path A→B passes through O. ✓

Part (b) — speed

AB⃗ = (12−(−24))i + (5−(−10))j = 36i + 15j

|AB⃗| = √(36² + 15²) = √(1296 + 225) = √1521 = 39 m

Speed = 39 m ÷ 4 s = 9.75 m s⁻¹

Q6 — harder: vector geometry in a triangle

Triangle OAB, C where OC⃗ = 2OA⃗, M midpoint of AB

6 marks

OA⃗ = a, OB⃗ = b. C is such that OC⃗ = 2OA⃗. M is midpoint of AB. Line CM cuts OB at N.

(a) Find CM⃗ in terms of a and b. (2 marks)

(b) Show that ON⃗ = (2 − 3λ/2)a + (λ/2)b. (2 marks)

(c) Hence prove ON : NB = 2 : 1. (2 marks)

Part (a) — find CM⃗

OC⃗ = 2a, so CA⃗ = OA⃗ − OC⃗ = a − 2a = −a

AM⃗ = ½AB⃗ = ½(b − a)

CM⃗ = CA⃗ + AM⃗ = −a + ½(b − a) = −a − ½a + ½b = −(3/2)a + (1/2)b

Part (b) — parametric form for ON⃗

N lies on line CM, so CN⃗ = λ · CM⃗ for some scalar λ.

ON⃗ = OC⃗ + CN⃗ = 2a + λ(−(3/2)a + (1/2)b)

= 2a − (3λ/2)a + (λ/2)b = (2 − 3λ/2)a + (λ/2)b ✓

Part (c) — find λ and prove ratio

N lies on OB, so the coefficient of a must be 0:

2 − 3λ/2 = 0 → λ = 4/3

ON⃗ = (4/3)(1/2)b = (2/3)b

NB⃗ = OB⃗ − ON⃗ = b − (2/3)b = (1/3)b

ON : NB = (2/3) : (1/3) = 2 : 1 ✓

Q7 — harder: collinear proof

P, Q, R collinear; Q is one third of the way from P to R

3 marks

Show that q = ⅓(r + 2p)

Q is ⅓ of way from P to R, so QR⃗ = ⅔ PR⃗

r − q = (2/3)(r − p)

r − q = (2/3)r − (2/3)p

q = r − (2/3)r + (2/3)p = (1/3)r + (2/3)p

q = (1/3)(r + 2p) ✓
